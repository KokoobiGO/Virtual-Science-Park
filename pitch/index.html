01 In BSP's words

“What is happening this week that is relevant? What should I act upon?”

Blue Science Park, challenge briefing, 13 Nov 2026

“There are a lot of test beds that can be used, but it is not that easy to find them for a single user.”

When BSP briefed us, this was the question their members ask. Not "give me more information". It is: what is relevant this week, and what should I do about it?

And on testbeds: there are many, but finding the right one for a single company is hard.

The information is public. The problem is that it is scattered: Vinnova, the EU portal, Energimyndigheten, regional funders, event pages, and 56 company websites.

Our own audit: Vinnova's testbed list has about 130 entries with no location and no contact. BSP's event pages show formats without dates.

And BSP's members are mostly small teams. Nobody's job is to watch all this.

01 The real cause

Access isn't the problem. Relevance is.

So we separated the symptoms from the causes. Calls get missed because there are nine sources in nine formats: so we built one store. Newsletters get ignored because they are not about you: so every member gets a profile. Personal tools fail because nobody fills in the questionnaire: so we build the profile first, from public pages, and the member only corrects it. And links don't get acted on: so every card ends in a next step.

[On entry] Two agents read the public sources: the Profiler reads company websites and writes facts with exact quotes; the collectors read funding, events and testbeds, one adapter per source.

[Press] Everything lands in one store. The database decides who may read what.

[Press] Reasoning agents only read the store: the matcher ranks, the judge keeps or rejects each match and writes why.

The brief asks for three kinds of request. A relevant one gets a cited answer that says "information only".

[Press] An unrelated one gets one fixed sentence that points to BSP staff.

[Press] An override attempt gets the same sentence, and the assistant is never even called. Three layers: a scope check, a read-only assistant, and an output check in code. Privacy is decided by the database, not by a prompt. We attacked it 44 ways: 44 of 44 held.

Things broke, and we changed them. With a stronger model the judge became too generous and kept 92% of matches, so we tightened the rule and judged everything again. Our own red team broke the Copilot three times; we fixed all three. Seven websites can't be read, so those profiles say they are thin.

And what we cannot show yet: that members act on it week after week. That is what the next step is for.

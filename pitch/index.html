We built Virtual Science Park: a weekly assistant for Blue Science Park's member companies.

It reads the members' own websites and the funding, testbed and event sources, and tells each member what may fit them this week. Every line shows where it came from.

We went after the causes, not the symptoms.

Why this problem: BSP named testbeds and funding as the two concrete pains, so that is where we proved it first. Events, trends and member connections reuse the same profile and matcher.

We separated what members see from why it happens. Members miss things because the sources are scattered. Generic lists get ignored because relevance depends on knowing the company. And personal tools fail because nobody fills in the questionnaire first.

So we fixed the causes: one store for all sources, a profile per company built from public pages before anyone types anything, and cards that end in a next step.

We started where BSP said it hurts most: testbeds and funding.

The quote check

  1. Quote copied word for word, in the page's own language. Only the value is in English.
  2. Both sides cleaned the same way: Unicode, spaces, quote marks, case. Nothing looser.
  3. Found in the stored page? Yes: kept, with link and date. No: one retry, then the field stays Missing.
314 / 419profile quotes found word for word on the page
85 / 448fields left Missing instead of guessed

What it cannot catch: a quote that is real but misread. “UV” here probably means underwater. That is why the member can correct every field.

Every fact on a profile carries a quote, word for word, in Swedish when the page is Swedish. Code checks that the quote is in the page we stored. If it is not, one retry, then the field stays Missing. We never guess.

And the honest limit: the check proves the quote is real, not that we read it right. "UV" here probably means underwater. So the member can correct every field, and a correction is never overwritten.

04 Honest limits

What we learned, and what does not work yet.

We found it, and changed it

A stronger model made the judge too generous

It kept 92% of ranked matches, some clearly unrelated. We tightened the rule and judged every match again.

A real quote can still be misread

“UV” on a Swedish page probably means underwater. So every field is editable, and a correction is never overwritten.

Our own red team broke the Copilot first

41 of 44 on the first run. We fixed the three failures and re-ran: 44 of 44.

Known limits

7 of 56 websites could not be read

Pages drawn by JavaScript come out empty; 6 profiles are thin and say so.

Relevance is not yet rated by BSP

The keep-or-reject line was set by us. Where it should sit is a question for BSP staff.

No proof yet that members act on it

A weekend cannot show whether members use it week after week. The pilot measures that.

Three things we found and changed: the judge got too generous with a stronger model, so we tightened it; a real quote can be misread, so every field is editable; our red team broke the Copilot first, 41 of 44, and we fixed it to 44 of 44.

And what does not work yet: some websites cannot be read, relevance is not rated by BSP yet, and we cannot yet show that members act on it week after week.
